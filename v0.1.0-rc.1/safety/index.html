Safety notes¶

What can go wrong with a gas flow instrument on a serial line, and what the driver does about it. Nothing here has been verified on an instrument: the behaviours the manual does not state are marked as assumptions on the Datasheet map.

Datasheet sections this page relies on

From Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-MANUAL-BASIS2 Rev 4:

  • Mounting, LED and power-up behaviour: Getting Started, Mounting, p. 5
  • Power and signal connections, pin 4 only: Power and Signal Connections, p. 6
  • Setpoint, setpoint source and ramp: Setpoint; Setpoint Source; Maximum Setpoint Ramp Rate, p. 11
  • Communication watchdog: Communication Watchdog, p. 12
  • Hold valve, gains, reference temperature, averaging: Hold Valve Position; Loop Control Gains; Reference Temperature; Readings Averaging, p. 13
  • Tare and autotare: Taring the Instrument; Using Ground to Tare; Autotare, p. 14
  • Unit ID: Unit ID, p. 16
  • Modbus address, baud rate, command protocol: Modbus Address; Serial Baud Rate; Command Protocol, p. 17
  • Analog signals: Analog Signals, p. 28

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

Power¶

Connect power to pin 4 only, and switch it on only when every other pin is connected (manual p. 6). Power on any other pin can permanently damage the instrument. The supply is 12 to 24 V; the manual states no absolute maximum, no reverse polarity protection and no ESD rating, so provide your own. Never connect the instrument to a loop-powered system: the manual says it will destroy parts of the circuitry (p. 28); use an isolator and a separate supply.

The valve of a controller¶

A controller's valve is normally closed. The driver offers four ways to move it, and each has a failure mode that the application must cover:

  • A setpoint (SetSetpoint()) is checked against 0 to 2.5 % over full scale, and read back unless a ramp is active (the instrument then lags on purpose).
  • A hold (HoldValve(percent), OpenValve(), CloseValve()) overrides the loop: the valve stays where it is put until ResumeControl(). The status flag HLD and a slow red blink of the LED show it. A held valve does not follow a setpoint, so a program that holds the valve and then crashes leaves it held. Close it on every exit path.
  • A ramp (SetRamp(), DisableRamp()) limits how fast the setpoint is followed. The unit of the rate is not defined in the manual; check it on the bench before relying on it.
  • Thermal management (VTM) pulses the valve by itself when a setpoint gives no flow. It is an override, not a fault.

When in doubt, SetSetpoint(0.0) then CloseValve(), in that order, and leave the instrument safe: no flow, no ramp. SetSetpoint(0.0) does not release a held valve (the HLD flag stays); CloseValve() does the closing. Over Modbus every hold call (HoldValve(), OpenValve(), CloseValve(), ResumeControl()) is Unsupported: there SetSetpoint(0.0) is the only way to close the valve. FactoryRestore() reverts the setpoint source to its factory value, which the manual does not state; if it is analog, serial setpoints are ignored until the source is set again.

The watchdog does not protect an ASCII program¶

SetWatchdog() forces a zero setpoint and closes the valve when no serial communication arrives within the time, but the manual says it acts only when the setpoint was set over Modbus and the source is unsaved digital. With ASCII, with the saved source or with the analog source it does nothing. The constexpr function Basis2::WatchdogProtects(protocol, source) is that truth table. Do not rely on the watchdog with ASCII. The application must stop commanding and then close the valve itself, and must treat a lost line as a reason to do so.

Saved setpoints wear memory¶

SetpointSource::SavedDigital writes every setpoint to non-volatile memory and the instrument returns to the last saved one at power-up. The manual warns against changing it more often than every few minutes. The driver refuses a change sooner than Config::saved_setpoint_min_interval_ms (180 seconds) with RateLimited. For anything that changes often, use SetpointSource::UnsavedDigital, and remember that a saved setpoint is what the valve does at the next power-up before any program runs.

Tare¶

Tare() makes the present reading the zero. Do it only with true zero flow through the instrument and only on the pure process gas (manual p. 15). A tare with flow running sets a wrong zero that every later reading inherits. A meter can also be tared by grounding pin 1 for at least 25 ms; the tare completes when the ground is removed.

Guarded commands¶

The bus assumes one instrument until the application calls Bus::SetDeviceCount(n) (the default is one). On a line with several instruments declare it before anything else: it is what makes ChangeUnitId(), SetModbusAddress(), ChangeBaud() and FactoryRestore() refuse and Recover() never adopt another unit.

Six commands can leave the instrument unreachable, or change what the instrument does for every later program. They cannot be called by accident: each takes a token whose type names the consequence.

Call Token Consequence
ChangeUnitId(id, ConfirmUnitIdChange{}) ConfirmUnitIdChange Another unit ID; connect one instrument at a time (the call refuses when the bus declares more)
SetModbusAddress(a, ConfirmAddressChange{}) ConfirmAddressChange Another Modbus address
ChangeBaud(b, ConfirmBaudChange{}) ConfirmBaudChange The reply comes at the new speed; the port must switch in time (needs SetBaud())
SwitchToProtocol1(ConfirmAbandonProtocol2{}) ConfirmAbandonProtocol2 The instrument speaks the old protocol, which the driver does not; every call is Unsupported afterwards
SwitchToProtocol2(ConfirmReturnToProtocol2{}) ConfirmReturnToProtocol2 Back to BASIS Protocol 2
FactoryRestore(ConfirmFactoryRestore{}) ConfirmFactoryRestore Unit ID, baud rate, Modbus address and protocol revert

If a guarded command may have changed the line settings and the driver cannot confirm it, the state becomes StateUnknown: every call returns that error until a successful Recover() finds the same instrument again, except a switch-off (SetSetpoint(0.0) and CloseValve()), which is still sent with the settings the driver holds and returns its own result. After an unconfirmed @=, baud change or FACTORY RESTORE that switch-off tries, in this order, the held setting, the setting the driver was changing to, and the factory defaults, adopts the first one that answers, and puts the held setting back when none does; a valve is never left open for doubt. Begin() does not end the doubt: it refuses while the state is unknown. Recover() polls the configured ID or address first, and after an unconfirmed baud change or factory restore it also tries the other speed (the one the driver was changing to, or 38400). Only when the bus declares a single instrument (Bus::DeviceCount() is one) does it scan the unit IDs A to Z over ASCII or the addresses 1 to 247 over Modbus; with several instruments it never adopts another unit. It then runs Begin() and compares the serial number, firmware and kind with those learned before: a different instrument is VerifyMismatch. On any failure the driver stays in StateUnknown and keeps its old ID; a Recover() that ends Busy (a line that never went quiet) leaves the state as it was, unless its scan had already found the instrument at another ID, address or speed: those settings are then kept, still in doubt. After FactoryRestore() power-cycle the instrument first; when its reply is lost the bus still stays closed for Config::restore_holdoff_ms from the send.

On a line declared to hold several instruments, ChangeUnitId(), SetModbusAddress(), ChangeBaud() and FactoryRestore() return GuardNotConfirmed before sending: an unconfirmed change could not be traced there, one instrument cannot move the speed its neighbours keep, and the factory ID A and address 1 collide with a neighbour. A switch-off in doubt there tries only another speed at the held ID and address, never another ID or address, and puts the held speed back afterwards whether or not it answered: the line stays at the speed of the neighbours. Recover() there polls only the held ID or address at the held speed.

A line that never goes quiet¶

A request waits for 3.5 quiet character times before it is sent. When the line is not quiet within one line wait (its reply wait without its own duration, such as a measurement window) because a streaming or babbling peer talks, the request ends with Busy and nothing is sent, a switch-off included. As nothing was sent no reply can come: the quarantine that follows ends as soon as the line is quiet, and at the latest after two line waits. A call that writes returns Busy only when its write was not sent: a later step of it (a verify, a read-back, a second write, a retry of a read) that meets a busy line returns Timeout, as for a lost reply. A call made only of reads may have sent its earlier reads before it returns Busy. A scan (ScanUnitIds(), ScanModbusAddresses()) stops at the first poll that meets such a line and returns Busy rather than a partial list, and Recover() then returns Busy and leaves the state as it was (a doubt stays). A guarded command that ends Busy leaves nothing in doubt. Call the switch-off again once the line is quiet, and find the peer that talks out of turn (Bus::StreamingSuspected()).

A reset the driver does not see¶

The manual documents no reset flag, so the driver cannot know that the instrument lost power or restarted between two calls and then answered normally. What the instrument does after a restart follows from the manual: the totalizer restarts from zero, an unsaved digital setpoint is lost, and a saved setpoint (the S source) is applied again. The driver does not compare a new total with the one before. An application that must notice a reset watches for a total that drops, a setpoint read that differs from the last one set, or a reply that did not come (Timeout), and then calls Recover(). Until it does, the driver trusts its own record of the setpoint source, gas and ramp. Reset detection belongs to the application; the driver keeps the saved-setpoint write history across Begin() and Recover(), and arms its rate limit whenever a write may have reached the instrument, even if the reply was lost.

Over Modbus the factory BuildWriteSingle() takes no guard flag: writes to the baud, address, ID, protocol and factory-restore registers are protected only by the token types of Basis2. A switch-off over Modbus after SwitchToProtocol1() is refused like every call (assumption: register 2053 may still answer, the manual does not say).

The driver never sends the broadcast prefix * and never sends a streaming command: a streaming peer is only detected, counted and ignored.
