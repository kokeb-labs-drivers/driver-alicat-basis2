Hardware¶

The connector, wiring for RS-232 and RS-485, power, LED, analog signals and size classes of the BASIS 2 meters and controllers, as the driver uses them. The instrument is a module with one six-pin connector, not a chip in a package, so the figures draw the connector as one row of pins.

Datasheet sections this page relies on
  • Models, ranges and options: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-MANUAL-BASIS2 Rev 4, Introduction, p. 3
  • Mounting, LED and power-up behaviour: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-MANUAL-BASIS2 Rev 4, Getting Started, Mounting, p. 5
  • Power and signal connections, pin 4 only: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-MANUAL-BASIS2 Rev 4, Power and Signal Connections, p. 6
  • Connector pins and serial levels: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-MANUAL-BASIS2 Rev 4, Power and Signal Connections (pin table), p. 7
  • Tare and autotare: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-MANUAL-BASIS2 Rev 4, Taring the Instrument; Using Ground to Tare; Autotare, p. 14
  • Analog signals: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-MANUAL-BASIS2 Rev 4, Analog Signals, p. 28
  • Supply current, accuracy and update rate: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-SPECS-BASIS Rev 19, Technical Data, p. 1
  • Size classes and dimensions: Alicat Scientific, Inc. DOC-SPECS-BASIS Rev 19, Dimensions, p. 3

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

Power on pin 4 only

Only connect power to pin 4, and do not switch the supply on until all the other pins are connected. Power on any other pin can permanently damage the instrument (manual pp. 6 and 28). Do not connect the instrument to a loop-powered system: it will destroy parts of the circuitry. Use an isolator and a separate supply.

Facts that are not verified

Nothing here has been checked on an instrument. A fact that no document states is marked unverified: the JST GH pitch, whether an unused signal pin may float, the transceiver, the terminations and the decoupling of the two circuits, and the maximum supply.

The connector¶

Figure description and sources

The BASIS 2 (B and BC series) seen from the top, in its JST GH 6-pin connector package. Pin 1 is marked by the dot; the numbers run counter-clockwise from it. Each pad is coloured by the pin's electrical type (legend below). Next to it is the pin's name and, in mono, the hook of the driver's port that drives or reads it; a pin without a hook is not used by the driver.

Drawn from: docs/hardware.yml; inc/alicat_basis2_uart_interface.hpp.

JST GH 6-pin connector, 6 pins (Power and Signal Connections, pin table, p. 7).

Pin Name Type (domain) Function Driver hook If unused Datasheet
1 A-SET 1 analog (SIGNAL) Analog setpoint input (controllers); on a meter, ground this pin for at least 25 ms to tare - Leave open Using Ground to Tare (Meters), p. 15
2 A-OUT 2 analog (SIGNAL) Analog flow output, 0 to 5 V or 4 to 20 mA by model - Leave open Analog Signals, p. 28
3 GND ground Ground, common to power, analog and serial - - Power and Signal Connections, pin table, p. 7
4 PWR power (PWR) Power in, 12 to 24 V. Power on any other pin can permanently damage the instrument - - Power and Signal Connections, p. 6
5 RX/B bus (SIGNAL) RS-232 receive (the instrument receives, unverified: the manual does not say whose side Rx names) or RS-485 B (+), by model Write() - Power and Signal Connections, pin table, p. 7
6 TX/A bus (SIGNAL) RS-232 transmit (the instrument transmits, unverified: the manual does not say whose side Tx names) or RS-485 A (-), by model Read() - Power and Signal Connections, pin table, p. 7

Source of this table and of the chip figures: docs/hardware.yml.

Pin 1 does two jobs by model: on a controller it is the analog setpoint input, on a meter grounding it for at least 25 ms tares the instrument, and the tare completes when the ground is removed (manual p. 15). The serial pins carry RS-232 or RS-485 levels, decided by the model, not by software; the manual describes no TTL option, so a microcontroller UART needs a level shifter (RS-232) or a transceiver (RS-485). The driver is level-agnostic: the port owns the physical layer.

The sheets disagree in a few cells. Revision 19 of the specification sheet (August 2026) is used here; revision 16 (March 2024) differs in dimension B of the smallest class (0.44 in against 0.53 in), in the height of the 5 to 20 SLPM class (1.83 in against 1.80 in) and in several cells of the 50 to 100 SLPM rows. Revision 19 titles itself "through 20 slpm" but lists ten ranges; whether the 50 and 100 SLPM models are still BASIS 2 is open. The process connection is ⅛ in NPT female or SAE 4 up to 2 SLPM and ⅜ in NPT female or SAE 6 from 5 SLPM. Mount the body horizontally with the flow arrow parallel to the ground, on two M3 x 0.5 holes 6 mm deep.

Wiring¶

RS-485 multidrop¶

Figure description and sources

A schematic. The MCU is on the left, each of its pins level with the chip pin it connects to; above each wire is the hook of the driver's port that drives or reads it. Supply pins point up to their supply and decoupling, ground pins down. Pins on the right carry the loads and the pins tied to a supply or ground; a cross marks a pin left open. A dot is a junction; wires that cross without a dot are not connected.

Drawn from: docs/hardware.yml; inc/alicat_basis2_uart_interface.hpp.

Up to 26 instruments share one line, each with its own unit ID A to Z (or Modbus address). RS-485 is half duplex on one pair: the transceiver's A line goes to pin 6 and B to pin 5. The manual describes neither the transceiver nor a termination; a 120 ohm resistor at each end of a long line is general practice. The port owns the direction control (Porting).

RS-232¶

Figure description and sources

A schematic. The MCU is on the left, each of its pins level with the chip pin it connects to; above each wire is the hook of the driver's port that drives or reads it. Supply pins point up to their supply and decoupling, ground pins down. Pins on the right carry the loads and the pins tied to a supply or ground; a cross marks a pin left open. A dot is a junction; wires that cross without a dot are not connected.

Drawn from: docs/hardware.yml; inc/alicat_basis2_uart_interface.hpp.

One instrument per port. The MCU's TX goes through the level shifter to pin 5, where the instrument receives, and pin 6 goes through it to the MCU's RX. Unverified: the manual's pin table lists "Rx" and "Tx" without saying whose side they name; check the wiring against an instrument before first power. Pin 3 is the common ground of power, analog and serial.

Serial settings¶

Setting Value
Format 38400 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit; 4800 to 115200 selectable
Unit ID One letter A to Z, default A, unique on the line; duplicates corrupt data
Idle before a command 3.5 characters after the carriage return; the instrument does not act earlier
Digital update rate Up to 176 Hz, depending on baud rate and system
Modbus Address 1 to 247 (register 45), the same line settings, functions 3, 6 and 16

Timing derived from these is on the Protocols page.

Power¶

Model Supply Current
Controller 12 to 24 V on pin 4 210 mA
Meter 12 to 24 V on pin 4 12 mA
Either, with a 4 to 20 mA analog output plus 40 mA

Allow one minute for warm-up, five for best accuracy. The manual states no absolute maximum supply, no reverse polarity protection and no ESD rating.

Analog signals¶

The analog output is 0 to 5 V or 4 to 20 mA, by model, not by software; zero flow is about 0.010 V or 4 mA and full scale 5.0 V or 20 mA. A controller's analog setpoint input is 0 to 5 V or 4 to 20 mA for 0 to 100 percent of full scale and is used only while the setpoint source is Analog (LSS a). The driver reads and writes none of them.

Status LED¶

LED Meaning
Solid green Power, no errors
Flashing green Serial activity
Fast red blink Mass flow, temperature or totalizer over range (MOV, TOV, OVR)
Slow red blink Valve override: a hold, or thermal management (HLD, VTM)

Operating limits¶

Quantity Value
Ambient and gas temperature 0 to 50 C
Pressure Meters 0 to 145 PSIG; controllers by valve (0 to 145, LV05 0 to 100, LV08 0 to 60); maximum common mode 145 PSIG
Valve Normally closed (controllers); thermal management pulses it when a setpoint gives no flow
Ingress, humidity IP40, 95 % non-condensing; a 50 micron in-line filter is suggested
Gases Air, Ar, CO2, N2, O2, N2O, H2, He, CH4
